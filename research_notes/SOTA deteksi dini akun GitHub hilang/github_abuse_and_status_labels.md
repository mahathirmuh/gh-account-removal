## KQ1. Which studies detect fake stars, spam, malicious repositories, abusive accounts, star farming, commit or activity farming, or coordinated malware distribution on GitHub or using GH Archive?

### Takeaway
GitHub abuse work is dominated by fake-star and promotion-market measurement (He et al. StarScout; Du et al.) and by repository-level malware identification (SourceFinder and successors). The most directly competing account-level work found is GitSec (Gong et al., CIKM 2019; TKDE 2023), a supervised classifier for malicious GitHub accounts; a later taxonomy (Cheng et al., 2026) covers issue spam and reputation farming on a small manually labelled set.

### Cited Findings

Peer-reviewed or archival research:

- **He et al., StarScout (fake stars).** arXiv v1 (18 Dec 2024) was titled "4.5 Million (Suspected) Fake Stars in GitHub: A Growing Spiral of Popularity Contests, Scams, and Malware" — [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459v1). v2 (6 Sep 2025) and the published version are retitled "Six Million (Suspected) Fake Stars in GitHub/on GitHub: A Growing Spiral of Popularity Contests, Spams, and Malware"; authors Hao He, Haoqin Yang, Philipp Burckhardt, Alexandros Kapravelos, Bogdan Vasilescu, Christian Kästner — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459). Published in ICSE '26 (48th IEEE/ACM ICSE), pp. 1469-1481, DOI 10.1145/3744916.3764531 — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3744916.3764531). v2 HTML read across the whole page with targeted extraction prompts.
  - Data: "a Google BigQuery replica of all GitHub events" (GH Archive) with "totaling 63.9 million users, 331 million repositories, 326 million stars", and detection over "all GitHub event data from July 2019 to December 2024" — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).
  - Method: two signatures. Low activity: "StarScout finds GitHub accounts with only one WatchEvent" "plus at most one additional event (e.g., ForkEvent) in the same repository on the same day". Lockstep: "StarScout implements CopyCatch", "a state-of-the-art algorithm that has been deployed at Facebook to detect fake Likes", seeking "clusters of ≥50 accounts and ≥10 repositories" with "a 30-day interval" within "interleaving six-month chunks". Postprocessing keeps repositories where "there is at least one single month in which it gets more than 50 fake stars and the percentage of fake stars exceeds 50%" and "the all-time percentage of fake stars (relative to all stars) exceeds 10%", and considers only repositories "with at least 50 stars suspected as fake" — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).
  - Results (v2): "In total, StarScout identified six million (suspected) fake stars across 26,254 repositories", of which "1.06 million are identified with the low activity signature" and "4.93 million with the lockstep signature"; after postprocessing, "StarScout identified 18,617 repositories with fake star campaigns" with "301k participating accounts (corresponding to 3.81 million fake stars)". The introduction's "6.0 million fake stars and 18,617 repositories" mixes the pre- and post-processing counts — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2). v1 counts differ: 4.53M stars across 22,915 repositories before postprocessing; 15,835 repositories, 278k accounts and 3.1M stars after — [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v1).
  - Validation: recall against the Stargazers Ghost Network set: "StarScout can detect 688 (81.23%) of the 847 repositories and 11,903 (75.95%) of the 15,672 involved GitHub accounts"; the same text also states "up to 82.23% recall" for repositories, an internal discrepancy noted by the extraction tool. "Precision is inherently harder to evaluate because there is no obvious way to examine whether a star is fake or not", and the "∼99%" and "∼180 false positive repositories" figures are a stated conditional, not a measured value — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2). Criterion validity via later deletion status is in KQ2.
  - Stated limitations: "the evidence from StarScout alone is insufficient for disciplinary action"; "it is possible that some of the StarScout identified repositories and accounts may still be false positives"; "Another limitation of StarScout is the use of ad-hoc heuristics and parameters"; "it is challenging to run thorough sensitivity tests on these parameters"; samples were "downsampled due to GitHub's API rate limit"; CopyCatch is "a randomized greedy algorithm with no completeness guarantees" — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).
- **Du et al., Understanding Promotion-as-a-Service on GitHub (ACSAC 2020; abstract only).** Kun Du, Hao Yang, Yubao Zhang, Haixin Duan, Haining Wang, Shuang Hao, Zhou Li, Min Yang; DOI 10.1145/3427228.3427258. Abstract: the authors "identified 1,023 suspected promotion accounts from the market", trained an SVM and applied it to "all active users extracted from GH Archive ranging from 2015 to 2019"; "In total, we detected 63,872 suspected promotion accounts"; "a hidden functionality in GitHub is abused to boost the reputation of an account by forging historical commits"; "We estimate that suspicious promoters could make a profit of $3.41M and $4.37M in 2018 and 2019, respectively." — [ACSAC 2020 program entry](https://acsac.org/2020/program/final/s152.html). Validation method, metrics and limitations could not be read (PDF unreadable with the fetch tool).
- **Gong et al., GitSec (CIKM 2019; abstract only).** Qingyuan Gong, Jiayun Zhang, Yang Chen, Qi Li, Yu Xiao, Xin Wang, Pan Hui, "Detecting Malicious Accounts in Online Developer Communities Using Deep Learning", CIKM '19, pp. 1251-1260, DOI 10.1145/3357384.3357971 — [Aalto research portal (abstract)](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/detecting-malicious-accounts-in-online-developer-communities-usin/); [BibTeX record](https://bibtex.github.io/CIKM-2019-GongZ00XWH.html). Abstract: GitSec combines profile features with two user-activity sequences, each processed by its own neural network, merged by attention, then a supervised classifier; on real GitHub user data it reports F1 0.922 and AUC 0.940 — [Aalto research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/detecting-malicious-accounts-in-online-developer-communities-usin/). Extended version: Gong, Liu, Zhang, Chen, Li, Xiao, Wang, Hui, IEEE TKDE 35(10):10633-10649, 2023, DOI 10.1109/tkde.2023.3237838, adding interaction graphs with GNNs and structural-hole theory, AUC 0.916 — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FTKDE.2023.3237838). Label details are Unverified (see KQ7).
- **Cheng et al., Weaponizing the Commons (JAWs 2026 workshop at ICSE 2026).** Yuli Cheng, Xiaoyu Zhang, Jiongchi Yu, Shiqing Ma, Chao Shen, Yang Liu — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17909). Data: 392 manually annotated instances "composed of 310 GitHub repositories and 82 user accounts" drawn from "prior academic studies, security reports, and gray literature". Taxonomy: Attention Hijacking (Fake Stars, Automatic Updates, Keyword Stuffing, Typo Squatting), Authority Fraud (Spoofed Contributor), Spam (Issue Spam), Reputation Manipulation (Reputation Farming, Fake Stats). Metrics: "F1-score exceeding 89%" on those 392 instances; Keyword Stuffing F1 93.2%, Fake Stats 93.6%, floor 89.5% for Issue Spam and Reputation Farming. Limitation: dataset "sufficient for validation but limited in scale". The paper does not use account status as a label or signal — [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17909).
- **Rokon et al., SourceFinder (RAID 2020; abstract only).** Md Omar Faruk Rokon, Risul Islam, Ahmad Darki, Evangelos Papalexakis, Michalis Faloutsos, RAID 2020, pp. 149-163. Evaluated on 97K GitHub repositories, 89% precision and 86% recall on a labelled set, 7,504 malware source-code repositories identified, 18 "professional" malware authors. The page does not mention deleted repositories — [USENIX page](https://www.usenix.org/conference/raid2020/presentation/omar).
- **Tania et al., Who is Creating Malware Repositories on GitHub and Why? (WWW '24 Companion; metadata and dataset record only).** Nishat Ara Tania, Md Rayhanul Masud, Md Omar Faruk Rokon, Qian Zhang, Michalis Faloutsos; DOI 10.1145/3589335.3651582 — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3589335.3651582). The dataset record reports about 14,000 users (counts sum to 14,267) classified by "large language model (LLM) analytics" as Malicious 3,339, Likely Malicious 3,354, Benign 7,574, with repositories following the SourceFinder methodology — [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/10806593). Paper text not read.
- **Gonzalez et al., Anomalicious (ICSE-SEIP 2021; abstract only).** Danielle Gonzalez, Thomas Zimmermann, Patrice Godefroid, Max Schaefer. Commit-log and metadata rule-based flagging of anomalous commits; on 15 malware-infected repositories it identified 53.33% of malicious commits while flagging fewer than 1% of commits for most repositories — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03846).
- **Szabó, Kovács, Kusper, Data cleaning and spam filtering methods in the TAWOS database (Annales Mathematicae et Informaticae 63:132-148, 2026; abstract page only).** DOI 10.33039/ami.2026.06.004. Issue-level, not account-level: a benchmark of "947 GitHub issues (481 spam/noise and 466 legitimate issues)" "collected from moderator-locked spam issues and legitimate issues from popular repositories"; "OwnMetrics obtains the highest accuracy, 91.0%, at threshold 75", best LLM configurations 89.2% (Gemma-3-4B) and 89.1% (Mistral-7B) — [MTAK record](https://real.mtak.hu/242826/).
- **Bae and Yagemann, Beyond Takedown (arXiv 2606.26291, June 2026; abstract only).** Go modules hosted on GitHub: scan of 12.3M index entries, 2,289 malicious versions; at least 99.4% of artifacts later classified GitHub-unobservable (removed or suspended) "remained retrievable via Go proxy"; after disclosure GitHub removed 684 malicious repositories — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26291).
- **Bot detection (adjacent; account-level identification, no abuse label).** Golzadeh, Decan, Legay, Mens (2021; the opened page lists related DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2021.110911 without naming the journal) built a manual ground truth of 5,000 accounts (527 bots) from PR and issue comments, weighted precision, recall and F1 of 0.98 on a 40% test split — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03303). Chidambaram, Decan, Mens: BIMBAS model and RABBIT tool (publication link DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2024.112287 on the researchr page), ground truth of "1035 bots and 1115 humans", features from "activity sequences"; the abstract page gives no numeric metrics — [researchr BotSE 2025 entry](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/botse-2025-papers/16/A-Bot-Identification-Model-and-Tool-Based-on-GitHub-Activity-Sequences). RABBIT MSR '24 tool paper: Chidambaram, Mens, Decan, DOI 10.1145/3643991.3644877 (PDF metadata only) — [PDF link](https://natarajan-chidambaram.github.io/papers/MSR2024_RABBIT_A_tool_for_identifying_bot_accounts_based_on_their_recent_GitHub_event_history.pdf).
- **Not GitHub, flag to avoid miscitation.** Wu et al., "Exposing the Hidden Layer: Software Repositories in the Service of SEO Manipulation" (ICSE 2025) studies npm, Docker Hub and NuGet, with 3,801,682 abusive packages; the page does not list GitHub among studied platforms — [researchr entry](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icse-2025/icse-2025-research-track/83/Exposing-the-Hidden-Layer-Software-Repositories-in-the-Service-of-SEO-Manipulation).
- **Vision paper on actor reputation.** Kalu et al., ARMS (ICSE-JAWs 2026): seven generic signals for contributor reputation; the abstract does not mention account status — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18760).

Security vendor and community sources (not peer-reviewed):

- **Check Point Research, Stargazers Ghost Network (Antonis Terefos, 24 July 2024).** Malware distribution-as-a-service: "more than 3,000 active Ghost accounts are part of the network", "more than 2,200 malicious repositories"; identified by repository-name patterns, cross-referencing starring accounts, and shared templates and short links. "Since May 2024, GitHub has taken down approximately 1559 repositories and their related GitHub accounts"; "the Release account is usually the first to be banned"; "we estimate the total profit to be approximately $100,000". Limitations: "It's not clear whether all those accounts were created by Stargazer Goblin for malicious purposes", and "The actual number of accounts performing various network operations is unclear, as the network is constantly evolving." — [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2024/stargazers-ghost-network/).
- **Dagster blog, Tracking the Fake GitHub Star Black Market (16 March 2023).** REST API plus BigQuery GH Archive (stars from 2022 onward). Labels from buying stars on a dummy repository; clustering method achieved 98% precision and 85% recall on that repository, 0.17% false positive rate on Dagster's own repository; "All of the profiles used to star our test repository have been deleted." — [Dagster blog](https://dagster.io/blog/fake-stars).
- **OpenSSF Siren advisory on reputation farming (24 June 2024, forwarded to oss-security).** Activity on "Long-closed and approved Pull-Requests and issues" lets accounts "pad their Github account reputation"; no counts given; marked "Yes for reputation farming" under active exploitation — [oss-security archive](https://seclists.org/oss-sec/2024/q2/304).
- **Kaspersky, The GitVenom campaign (Georgy Kucherin, Joao Godinho, Securelist, 24 Feb 2025).** "hundreds of repositories on GitHub" with fake projects; the post does not say whether GitHub removed them — [Securelist](https://securelist.com/gitvenom-campaign/115694/).

### Inferences
- Fake-star and malware-repository work uses repository and campaign units; account-level classification is rarer. On the sources found, GitSec is the closest account-level precedent for the project (inference from its abstract and the partly Unverified label details).
- Commit and activity farming is documented mainly in grey literature and as a sub-category of Du et al. (forged historical commits) and Cheng et al. (Reputation Farming, Fake Stats), not as a dedicated large-scale study (inference from the sources opened).

### Gaps
- No dedicated peer-reviewed study of commit or contribution-graph farming, or of Hacktoberfest spam, was found in the searches listed (terms: "GitHub activity farming contribution graph fake commits airdrop Sybil detection study", "Hacktoberfest spam pull requests empirical study GitHub automated spam detection").
- Du et al., GitSec, Tania et al. and the RABBIT and BIMBAS papers could not be read in full (PDFs unreadable by the fetch tool); their validation details and limitations are incomplete here.

## KQ2. Has any work used current or later GitHub account or repository status (deleted, suspended, 404, 451, banned) as a label or as validation?

### Takeaway
Yes, as validation at both repository and account level: He et al. checked GitHub API accessibility of flagged accounts and repositories and compared deletion rates against random baselines. GitSec reportedly used GitHub's own annotation of malicious accounts as ground truth (only search-excerpt evidence). No study was found that uses disappearance after a long fixed horizon as a training label resolved by numeric ID.

### Cited Findings
- **He et al., deletion as criterion validity.** "we estimate criterion validity", "we compare the percentage of GitHub repositories and accounts that have been deleted" "(as of January 2025)", and "we check (using the GitHub API) whether they were still accessible at the time of detection", justified "based on reports of GitHub actively countering fraudulent and malicious activities" and the expectation that "we should be able to observe a higher deletion rate in the StarScout sample compared to the baseline". The baseline: "We also randomly sampled another 10,000 GitHub repositories with ≥50 stars and 10,000 GitHub stargazers". Results (Table 1): "90.42% of repositories and 57.07% of accounts in fake star campaigns" deleted, "Compared with the baseline deletion ratio (5.03% for repositories and 3.54% for users)" — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2). v1 reported 90.75% and 61.95% against baselines of 5.84% and 4.43%, "as of Oct 2024" — [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v1).
  - Entity: repositories and accounts flagged by StarScout plus random baselines. Gap: not fixed; a single snapshot around January 2025 (the text also says "at the time of detection", and Finding 7 refers to March 2025, as flagged by the extraction tool), for events spanning July 2019 to December 2024, so the status gap is event-dependent. Renames and numeric IDs: not addressed in Section 3.4 (no occurrence of "renamed", "rename", "numeric" or "user id"), and the paper does not say whether accounts are keyed by login or numeric ID. What API response counted as deleted is not stated. Noise: deletion is not split into platform removal versus self-deletion; the authors write "We speculate that GitHub may routinely take down known fake accounts or malware repositories" and note "GitHub does not preserve deleted repositories and users"; no statistical test is reported in Section 3.4 — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).
- **GitSec (CIKM 2019).** Search excerpts of the PDF state that GitHub-annotated malicious accounts were adopted as ground truth (details under Unverified in KQ7). The abstract page does not describe the labels — [Aalto research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/detecting-malicious-accounts-in-online-developer-communities-usin/).
- **Check Point** describes bans descriptively: "Since May 2024, GitHub has taken down approximately 1559 repositories and their related GitHub accounts", and notes "we do not have access to all the starred repositories as old ones were taken down". No gap or label use; the bans are context, not ground truth — [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2024/stargazers-ghost-network/).
- **Dagster** used purchased stars as labels and only anecdotally reported that "All of the profiles used to star our test repository have been deleted" within 48 hours of publishing — [Dagster blog](https://dagster.io/blog/fake-stars).
- **Bae and Yagemann** use "GitHub-unobservable" (removed or suspended) as a status category for malicious Go module artifacts; repository level; abstract only — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26291).
- **Moderation action as issue-level label.** Szabó et al. collected spam issues from "moderator-locked spam issues" — [MTAK record](https://real.mtak.hu/242826/).
- **Cross-platform lineage.** Thomas, Grier, Paxson, Song, "Suspended Accounts in Retrospect" (IMC 2011) identified over 1.1 million accounts Twitter suspended over seven months, with 77% of spam accounts suspended within one day of their first tweet — [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/suspended-accounts-in-retrospect-an-analysis-of-twitter-spam/). TrollMagnifier (Saeed et al., arXiv 2112.00443, shorter version in IEEE S&P 2022) checks Reddit status as validation: "If the account was suspended, we get a 403 HTTP error; if it was deleted, the HTTP error code is 404."; "298 out of the 1,248 accounts were either suspended or deleted"; status is "an additional indicator of troll behavior" and negatives are filtered so "that these are not suspended by Reddit"; the time gap between collection and status check is not stated — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.00443).

### Inferences
- The idea of using later platform status on GitHub is already in print as a validation device at account level (He et al.); status-as-ground-truth for training has precedent on GitHub only through GitSec's annotation-based labels (pending verification) and has a long lineage on Twitter and Reddit. This is inference from the listed sources.
- Reddit separates suspended from deleted by HTTP status (TrollMagnifier); GitHub's documented user-lookup responses do not (KQ5). On GitHub, the 404 label is therefore a mixture that prior work has not decomposed.

### Gaps
- No study was found that treats disappearance after a fixed long horizon (for example 31 months) as a label and resolves accounts by numeric ID to avoid rename false positives; not found in the searches listed (terms: "GH Archive deleted accounts 404 GitHub API label fake account detection study", "detecting spam or malicious GitHub accounts machine learning suspended accounts label", "GitHub malicious repositories detection empirical study removed by GitHub deleted repositories label", "GitHub spam accounts dataset "no longer exist" deleted accounts validation empirical study fake followers sockpuppets repositories", "GitHub login change user ID rename tracking dataset "user id" stable identity disambiguation mining study").
- The precise date of He et al.'s status check and the response that counted as deleted remain unclear.

## KQ3. Has any work predicted account-level disappearance, suspension or deletion on GitHub? Separate this from churn or turnover prediction.

### Takeaway
Not found in the searches listed for GitHub; the closest GitHub account-level work is GitSec, which predicts malicious status rather than disappearance. Developer churn work targets leaving a project or inactivity, not platform account disappearance, and is a different problem.

### Cited Findings
- A search for GitHub suspension or deletion prediction returned only community posts and anecdotes, with no model or study — search terms in Gaps.
- **Closest GitHub account-level classifier:** GitSec (see KQ1), which targets malicious versus legitimate accounts — [Aalto research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/detecting-malicious-accounts-in-online-developer-communities-usin/).
- **Closest prediction of disappearance on other platforms:** Volkova and Bell, "Identifying Effective Signals to Predict Deleted and Suspended Accounts on Twitter across Languages" (ICWSM 2017, pp. 290-298), abstract summary: predicts "the removal or shutdown of an account", comparing deleted with active accounts in Russian, Spanish and English — [PNNL page](https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/identifying-effective-signals-predict-deleted-and-suspended-accounts-twitter-across). Shevtsov et al., "Russo-Ukrainian War: Prediction and explanation of Twitter suspension" (arXiv 2306.03502; related DOI 10.1145/3625007.3627317): 107.7M tweets from 9.8 million users, SHAP explanation of suspension; label definition not on the abstract page — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03502).
- **Churn and turnover (separate problem).** Bao, Xia, Lo, Murphy, "A Large Scale Study of Long-Time Contributor Prediction for GitHub Projects" (ICSE 2020 Journal-First listing): GHTorrent, 917 projects, 75,046 contributors, long-time contributor defined by first-to-last commit span T of 1, 2 or 3 years (9,238, 3,968 and 1,577 such contributors), 63 features "from a developer's first month of activity", random forest AUC above 0.75 — [researchr entry](https://2020.icse-conferences.org/details/icse-2020-Journal-First/72/A-Large-Scale-Study-of-Long-Time-Contributor-Prediction-for-GitHub-Projects) (abstract only). Calefato, Gerosa, Iaffaldano, Lanubile, Steinmacher, "Will You Come Back to Contribute? Investigating the Inactivity of OSS Core Developers in GitHub" (Empirical Software Engineering 27(3), 2022, DOI 10.1007/s10664-021-10012-6): 18 OSS organizations; about 45% of core developers fully disengaged for at least a year; returning from a break has roughly a 35-55% chance, dropping to about 21-26% for breaks of a year or longer — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04656) (abstract only).

### Inferences
- Churn studies measure disengagement from a project while the account persists; the project's target (account gone from the platform at T+31 months) is a distinct label, and the churn literature provides features, not a competing prior for C1 (inference).

### Gaps
- Not found in the searches listed: a GitHub study predicting account suspension or deletion (terms: "predicting GitHub account suspension deletion prediction developers banned accounts", "detecting spam or malicious GitHub accounts machine learning suspended accounts label").

## KQ4. Has any work done this kind of detection from a single short window of GH Archive, or analyzed performance as a function of window length on GitHub data?

### Takeaway
Not found in the searches listed. The nearest evidence concerns the amount of activity (number of comments or events per account), not wall-clock window length, and uses per-account history rather than a public-stream snapshot.

### Cited Findings
- **Golzadeh et al.:** performance as a function of the number of non-empty comments per account, "The F1-score increases from 0.87 (bin 0-4) and becomes stable around 0.96 to 1.00 after 10 non-empty comments"; the authors "cannot claim that our model applies on commenters who made fewer than 10 comments" and "considered at most 100 comments for each commenter". Data came from the GraphQL API with comments extracted in February 2020, covering 17 December 2009 to 15 February 2020 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03303).
- **RABBIT:** "fetches the latest public events using the GitHub Events API", default `--min-events` 5 and `--max-queries` 3 (range 1-3), returning "Unknown" below the threshold; non-existent logins return "Invalid" — [RABBIT README](https://github.com/natarajan-chidambaram/RABBIT). Inference uses per-account API calls, and no event-count or window sensitivity analysis could be read (paper not opened).
- **StarScout:** uses a 30-day interval over six-month chunks across multi-year GH Archive data; the authors state "it is challenging to run thorough sensitivity tests on these parameters" — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).
- **Bao et al.:** features from a developer's first month of activity predict long-time contribution; a single fixed early window with no window sweep described in the abstract — [researchr entry](https://2020.icse-conferences.org/details/icse-2020-Journal-First/72/A-Large-Scale-Study-of-Long-Time-Contributor-Prediction-for-GitHub-Projects).

### Inferences
- If C3 were tested against the event-count literature, the expected finding is qualitatively similar to Golzadeh et al. (performance rises with observed activity and flattens); the unit (minutes of public stream, 5-60 min) and target (later disappearance) differ (inference).

### Gaps
- Not found in the searches listed: any study that sweeps wall-clock windows on GH Archive for account-level detection or prediction (terms: "first hours short time window early detection GitHub accounts spam stars events stream prediction window length", "anomaly detection GH Archive event stream hourly window account-level classification malicious GitHub actors real-time").
- Full texts of GitSec, Du et al. and the BIMBAS paper could not be opened; they may contain sensitivity analyses that were not visible in abstracts.

## KQ5. What does GitHub publicly document about suspended and deleted accounts, API behaviour (404 vs 451, DMCA), research use under the Acceptable Use Policies, and how much disappearance is suspension rather than self-deletion?

### Takeaway
GitHub documents a durable numeric user-ID lookup returning 200 or 404, and 451 only for DMCA-blocked repositories and gists; no opened GitHub page states how suspended or hidden accounts respond to the API, and no published split of disappearance into suspension versus self-deletion was found. The AUP permits research use of public, non-personal information, conditional on open-access publication.

### Cited Findings
- **Numeric-ID lookup.** "Get a user using their ID" (`GET /user/{account_id}`): "This method takes their durable user ID instead of their login, which can change over time." Documented statuses for both user-lookup endpoints are 200 and 404. For Enterprise Managed Users, "If you are not authorized, the request will return a 404 Not Found status." The page does not mention deleted or suspended accounts — [REST users docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/users/users?apiVersion=2022-11-28#get-a-user-using-their-id).
- **Renames.** "After changing your username, your old username becomes available for anyone else to claim."; links to the previous profile "will return a 404 error"; the page does not mention user IDs — [Username changes docs](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/concepts/username-changes).
- **Deletion.** Deleting a personal account removes owned repositories, forks of private repositories, wikis, issues, pull requests and Pages sites; the username "will be available for anyone to use after 90 days"; the only "ghost" sentence on that page concerns comments that "will be associated with the `ghost` user" in the account-conversion context — [Personal account reference](https://docs.github.com/en/account-and-profile/reference/personal-account-reference). "Once your personal account has been deleted, GitHub cannot restore your content." — [Deleting your account](https://docs.github.com/en/articles/deleting-your-user-account).
- **Suspension and termination.** "GitHub has the right to suspend or terminate your access to all or any part of the Website at any time", "with or without cause, with or without notice, effective immediately."; on data, "we will delete your full profile and the Content of your repositories within 90 days of cancellation or termination" "(though some information may remain in encrypted backups)" — [Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service). Account rules: "One person or legal entity may maintain no more than one free Account"; "Accounts registered by "bots" or other automated methods are not permitted." — [Terms of Service](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service).
- **Appeals.** Enforcement actions include "Suspend or terminate access to an account" and "Remove, disable access to, or restrict visibility of content"; "On GitHub, you may seek Reinstatement or Appeal a moderation decision for up to six months following the decision." — [Appeal and Reinstatement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-appeal-and-reinstatement).
- **451.** "The GitHub API will now respond with a `451` status code for resources it has been asked to take down due to a DMCA notice", scoped to "repositories and gists"; previously "`403 - Forbidden`"; the page does not mention users or accounts (17 March 2016) — [GitHub API changes](https://developer.github.com/changes/2016-03-17-the-451-status-code-is-now-supported).
- **Moderation scale (2022 report).** "In 2022, we hid 12,860 accounts and reinstated 480 hidden accounts."; "We restricted a repository owner's access to 428 accounts and reinstated it for 58 accounts."; 8,822 accounts had both measures; chart totals: 23,175 abuse-related restrictions remained in place (97.2%), 653 reinstated (2.74%), 25 partially reinstated (0.105%) — [GitHub 2022 Transparency Report](https://github.blog/news-insights/policy-news-and-insights/2022-transparency-report). GitHub's public moderation vocabulary thus includes "hidden" accounts, separate from deletion — same source. The September 2026 policy post gives government takedown counts only (98 in 2025, 708 in H1 2026, largely a reporting-methodology change) and no account-restriction figures — [GitHub policy update](https://github.blog/news-insights/policy-news-and-insights/developer-policy-update-transparency-state-policy-and-whats-ahead/). The Transparency Center data repository lists folders for appeals and reinstatements but the page gave no account-restriction definitions — [github/transparency](https://github.com/github/transparency).
- **Flagged accounts (community, not official).** Users report accounts "flagged" and hidden from public view, without staff explanation of mechanism; the thread does not mention 404 — [GitHub Community #27294](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/27294).
- **Acceptable Use Policies, research use.** Section 7: "You may use information from our Service for the following reasons, regardless of whether the information was scraped,"... "Researchers may use public, non-personal information from the Service for research purposes," "only if any publications resulting from that research are "open access"". "Scraping does not refer to the collection of information through our API." Spam use is barred: information may not be used "for spamming purposes, including for the purposes of sending unsolicited emails to users or selling personal information,". Section 4 lists prohibited "inauthentic interactions, such as fake accounts and automated inauthentic activity;" and "rank abuse, such as automated starring or following;". Section 8: "Misuse of personal information is prohibited."; "If you collect any personal information from the Service, you agree that you will only use that personal information" "for the purpose for which that User has authorized it." — [GitHub AUP](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies).
- **Base rate of account disappearance.** The only empirical estimate found: He et al.'s random baseline of 10,000 stargazers, 3.54% deleted as of January 2025 in v2 and 4.43% in v1 as of Oct 2024 (not directly comparable, different versions and samples) — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2), [v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v1).

### Inferences
- A 404 from `GET /user/{id}` is documented only as "not found"; GitHub's documentation does not distinguish self-deletion from suspension or hiding, so the project's 404 label is a mixture of causes, and hidden or flagged accounts are a plausible but undocumented additional source (community reports only).
- The appeals and reinstatement pathway means some labels can reverse; the 2.74% reinstatement rate of abuse-related restrictions in 2022 is the only official reversal figure found, in mixed units and not a false-positive rate (inference).
- Username re-registration (90 days after deletion; immediately after a rename) makes login-based lookups unsafe; the numeric-ID lookup is the documented alternative (inference from the REST, rename and deletion docs).
- Compliance notes for the project (inference): publish open access per AUP Section 7, avoid storing personal fields beyond what the labelling needs per Section 8.

### Gaps
- No GitHub page found states how a suspended or hidden personal account responds to `GET /users/{login}` or `GET /user/{id}`; no opened page confirms that suspended accounts return 404 (searches: "GitHub account deleted or suspended API returns 404 ghost user deleted account what happens docs", "GitHub flagged account hidden profile 404 suspended account public profile").
- Not found in the searches listed: a published estimate of the share of account disappearance due to suspension versus self-deletion (terms: "GitHub Transparency Center abuse takedowns account suspensions number report", plus the two searches above).
- The Transparency Center front page is rendered by JavaScript; the fetch returned only its title.

## KQ6. What are the known limitations of GH Archive: missing events, coverage gaps, PushEvent truncation, schema or payload changes, and 2025-2026 Events API changes?

### Takeaway
GH Archive is a best-effort single-page poller of a lossy, delayed API; missing events and missing hours are documented in issue reports, and event capture appears to collapse from May 2025 onward. The project's 2024-04-01 15:00 UTC window predates the 2025-2026 collapses and the October 2025 payload trimming, but falls in the regime with first-page-only polling.

### Cited Findings
- **Official scope.** "Activity archives are available starting 2/12/2011."; data for "2/12/2011-12/31/2014 was recorded from the (now deprecated) Timeline API", and from "1/1/2015 is recorded from the Events API"; `payload` content "may be updated by GitHub at any point"; "if GitHub adds a new field, it will show up in "other" until and unless the schema is extended to support it."; the page does not mention gaps — [gharchive.org](https://www.gharchive.org/).
- **Crawler design.** Requests `https://api.github.com/events?per_page=#{PAGE_LIMIT}` with `PAGE_LIMIT = 500` and no `page` parameter, rescheduling with `EM.add_timer(0.75, &process)`, comparing IDs to the previous poll — [crawler.rb](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/blob/master/crawler/crawler.rb). The REST API documents a maximum of 100 results per page and "up to 300 events", with only the past 30 days available, and "This API is not built to serve real-time use cases."; "event latency can be anywhere from 30s to 6h" — [Events API docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/activity/events?apiVersion=2022-11-28).
- **Missing events and files (issue reports, unverified by maintainers).** #245 (2021) reports missing files: 2019-09-12-8 to 2019-09-13-5 (21 hours), 2020-06-10-12 to -21 (10 hours), 2020-08-21-9 to 2020-08-23-15 (55 hours) — [#245](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/245). #294 (Nov 2023) reports PR and issue events missing from October 2023 files and a repository "missed" in `2024-02-29-15.json`; a commenter attributes this to first-page-only polling, 100 events per page, 300 total; the owner replied only that he "would appreciate any help!" — [#294](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/294).
- **May 2025 step-down (issue reports).** #310: "Drastic Drop Off in Events After 2025-05-23": hourly file for 2025-05-23 00:00 had 200,297 events versus 136,470 on 2025-05-24; daily archive size falling from about 2.4G to 1.3G, and to 5.1M on 2025/10/09; 936,153 "missing" IDs in 2025-05-23 — [#310](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/310). #320: WatchEvent capture fell from roughly 90-100% to 15.8% in 2026-02 and 7.7% in 2026-03 for `google/osv.dev`; aggregate ratio from 0.90 to 0.71 at onset on 2025-05-24 and about 0.01 of expected in 2026-07/08; no maintainer reply; cause not established — [#320](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/320).
- **October 2026 collapse (issue report).** #326: "~3k events per hour since 2026-10-27 (normal is 60k-170k)"; 2026-06-15-12 had 162,822 events, 2026-10-15-12 had 3,191, 2026-10-27-12 had 2,943; reporter attributes causes to the October 2025 payload change, an event-ID restart around 2025-10-09, a single-page collector, and the 2026-06-30 stargazer restriction; unconfirmed, no maintainer reply — [#326](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/326). #323 announces an OpenDigger mirror from 2026-09-06, polling all three API pages, cannot guarantee completeness, "~8x the events" for one sample hour per a commenter — [#323](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues/323). Other open issues by title only: #278 "Different repoIds of the same repository" and #296 duplicate PullRequestReviewEvents — [issue list](https://github.com/igrigorik/gharchive.org/issues).
- **PushEvent truncation.** Older documentation (Enterprise Server 3.14): the `commits` array "includes a maximum of 20 commits", "If necessary, you can use the Commits API to fetch additional commits.", "This limit is applied to timeline events only and isn't applied to webhook deliveries.", alongside `size` and `distinct_size` — [GHES 3.14 event types](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-server@3.14/rest/using-the-rest-api/github-event-types). Current documentation lists PushEvent fields `repository_id`, `push_id`, `ref`, `head`, `before` and no commits array; `actor` has `id`, `login`, `display_login`, `gravatar_id`, `url`, `avatar_url` — [current event types](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/using-the-rest-api/github-event-types?apiVersion=2022-11-28).
- **2025 payload change.** Changelog of 8 August 2025: "Commit summaries and counts" removed from push events, fields trimmed from pull request events, `author_association` removed from Pull Request, Pull Request Review, Pull Request Review Comment, Issue, Issue Comment, Commit Comment and Discussion events; brownout 8 September 2025, implementation 7 October 2025; GitHub said events previously could take up to eight hours to appear and that removed fields remain available via REST — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-08-08-upcoming-changes-to-github-events-api-payloads/).
- **2024-2025 retention change.** "On January 30th, 2025, we will be modifying the window of data retention for these features from 90 days to 30 days." (Events API, Atom feed, /timeline, /dashboard-feed) — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2024-11-08-upcoming-changes-to-data-retention-for-events-api-atom-feed-timeline-and-dashboard-feed-features/).
- **2026 access restrictions.** Stargazer and watcher list endpoints limited to admins and collaborators, because "this information has increasingly been misused to collect user data for spam activities"; `GET /users/{username}`, `GET /user/{id}` and the Events API are not mentioned — [GitHub Changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-30-upcoming-access-restrictions-to-public-api-endpoints-and-ui-views/).

### Inferences
- The project's window (2024-04-01 15:00-15:59 UTC) is after the known 2023-2024 first-page-only missing-event reports but before the May 2025 step-down and October 2025 trimming, so the 2024 payload is intact; first-page polling implies that bursts can be lost non-randomly, and bursty accounts plausibly include automated and abusive accounts (inference), which affects C3 because a 5-minute window has fewer events to absorb losses.
- Replication on post-May-2025 windows would be confounded by the reported capture collapse; the 2024 window should be held as the reference (inference).

### Gaps
- All coverage figures from issue threads are reporter claims, not validated by the GH Archive maintainer; no peer-reviewed study of GH Archive completeness was found in the searches listed (terms: "GH Archive missing events data quality coverage gaps study").
- No opened source quantifies the event loss rate for the 2024-04-01 hour.

## Verdict on C1 and C3

### Takeaway
**C1: partially defeated.** Later GitHub platform status has been used at account and repository level as validation (He et al., ICSE '26, closest prior work; 57.07% of campaign accounts deleted vs a 3.54% baseline), and platform moderation annotation has reportedly been used as account-level ground truth (Gong et al., GitSec, Unverified details). The general idea cannot be claimed as new; retrospective status labels also have over a decade of precedent on Twitter (Thomas et al., 2011) and on Reddit (TrollMagnifier). What was not found in the searches listed is the specific combination: a fixed long horizon (about 31 months), numeric-ID resolution to exclude renames, and treatment of still-present accounts as unlabeled. **C3: not defeated by the sources found, but only because no window-length sweep on GitHub data was found in the searches listed**; the closest related analyses are activity-count curves (Golzadeh et al., 2021) and public-stream detectors with long windows (He et al.; Du et al.). This is absence of evidence from the listed searches, not evidence that it has never been done.

### Cited Findings
- C1 evidence: He et al. status check and baselines — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2); GitSec abstract — [Aalto research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/fi/publications/detecting-malicious-accounts-in-online-developer-communities-usin/); Twitter retrospective precedent — [Thomas et al. page](https://research.google/pubs/suspended-accounts-in-retrospect-an-analysis-of-twitter-spam/); Reddit status validation — [TrollMagnifier on ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.00443).
- C1 numeric-ID detail: GitHub documents the numeric lookup as using a "durable user ID instead of their login, which can change over time" — [REST users docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/users/users?apiVersion=2022-11-28#get-a-user-using-their-id).
- C3 evidence: activity-count analysis — [Golzadeh et al. on ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.03303); long-window public-stream detector and its stated difficulty with sensitivity tests — [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13459v2).

### Inferences
- Recommended reframing of C1: present retrospective platform status as an established but noisy proxy (citing He et al., GitSec, Thomas et al.), and claim only the concrete methodological refinements: numeric-ID rename-safe resolution, a fixed long horizon, and a positive-unlabeled treatment of present accounts. The numeric-ID lookup itself is a documented API feature, so it should be presented as careful practice rather than a contribution in its own right.
- Recommended framing of C3: an empirical measurement of how detection degrades with sub-hour public-stream windows; a reviewer may regard window sensitivity as a standard robustness analysis unless the paper shows a substantive finding (for example, a usable performance floor at 5 minutes). Event-count curves from Golzadeh et al. are the natural related-work contrast.
- Any C1 claim about what 404 means needs the caveat that GitHub does not document suspended or hidden accounts' API behaviour, and GitSec excerpts suggest blocked accounts remained API-visible as of 2019 (Unverified).

### Gaps
- What would change this verdict: reading the full text of GitSec (label mechanism and horizon), Du et al. (any status-based validation), and BIMBAS (event-count sensitivity); these PDFs could not be opened and should be checked by hand before the novelty claims are finalised.
- A targeted search of Crossref, DBLP and Semantic Scholar listings for "suspended" or "deleted" with "GitHub" in 2026 preprints may surface newer items; the search tools used here returned few scholarly listings for those terms.
