## Q1. State of the art in PU learning: assumptions, 2021-2026 advances, maintained Python implementations

### Takeaway
SCAR methods (Elkan & Noto 2008; nnPU 2017) remain the baselines every PU paper reports, but they assume labeling is independent of features given the class, which enforcement that favours visible accounts violates. Selected-at-random (SAR) labeling (Bekker et al. 2019) is the right formal setting, and it is only identifiable through restrictions on the propensity function. Evidence through 2026 rests mainly on image and small UCI benchmarks, the first systematic PU benchmark (ICLR 2026) found no overall winner, and maintained SAR tooling for tabular data is thin.

### Cited Findings

**Foundations under SCAR**
- Elkan & Noto, "Learning classifiers from only positive and unlabeled data," KDD 2008, DOI 10.1145/1401890.1401920 ([Crossref record](https://doi.org/10.1145/1401890.1401920); [full text](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/posonly.pdf)) [FT]. Assumption: selected completely at random, p(s=1|y=1,x) = p(s=1|y=1) = c, and p(s=1|y=0) = 0. Lemma 1: p(y=1|x) = p(s=1|x)/c, so a classifier g trained on labeled vs unlabeled ranks examples the same way as the true posterior f, and g ≤ c. The paper works in the single-training-set scenario, which it says provides strictly more information than case-control and is the only one in which p(y=1) can be estimated. Estimators of c: e1 = average of g over labeled validation examples (preferred, lowest variance), e2 = Σ_P g / Σ_V g, e3 = max_V g. Results (Table 1, identifying proteins for the incomplete TCDB database vs SwissProt, linear SVM base learner): (i) ideal, training on P ∪ Q vs N: accuracy 0.9600, F1 0.9472, AUC 0.9912; P vs U 0.9497, 0.9308, 0.9895; P vs weighted U 0.9568, 0.9422, 0.9899; biased SVM 0.9465, 0.9279, 0.9895.
- Bekker & Davis, "Learning from positive and unlabeled data: a survey," *Machine Learning* 109(4):719-760, 2020, DOI 10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5 ([Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5); [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820)) [FT]. Defines class prior α = Pr(y=1), label frequency c = Pr(s=1|y=1), propensity e(x) = Pr(s=1|y=1,x). Defines SCAR, SAR (bias "completely depends on the attributes"), and probabilistic gap PU. Under SCAR the non-traditional classifier preserves ranking and Pr(y=1|x) = Pr(s=1|x)/c. Distinguishes single-training-set from case-control data. Method families discussed: two-step reliable-example extraction, biased learning (§5.2), and direct class-prior incorporation (§5.3). When the labeling mechanism is unknown, "additional assumptions are needed to enable learning."
- Kiryo, Niu, du Plessis & Sugiyama, "Positive-Unlabeled Learning with Non-Negative Risk Estimator," NIPS 2017 oral per the arXiv comment ([arXiv:1703.00593](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00593)) [FT]. Two-sample (case-control) setting with πp "assumed known throughout the paper." Estimator: R̃pu(g) = πp·R̂p+(g) + max{0, R̂u−(g) − πp·R̂p−(g)}. Tested on MNIST, epsilon, 20News, CIFAR-10 with Table 2 priors πp = 0.49, 0.50, 0.44, 0.40 and np = 1,000. Prior misspecification was tested at πp' in {0.8, ..., 1.2}·πp: on MNIST, more misspecification was worse and under-misspecification hurt more than over-misspecification. Code: [kiryor/nnPUlearning](https://github.com/kiryor/nnPUlearning), written in Chainer.

**Biased labeling (SAR and related)**
- Bekker, Robberechts & Davis, "Beyond the Selected Completely at Random Assumption for Learning from Positive and Unlabeled Data," ECML PKDD 2019, LNCS pp. 71-85, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-46147-8_5 ([Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-46147-8_5); [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03207)) [FT]. Assumption: labeling probability is a function of attributes, e(x). Propensity-weighted estimator gives each labeled example weight 1/e_i and adds a compensating negative-weight term 1 − 1/e_i; it is unbiased when e is known. With estimated e, bias depends on accuracy for positive examples, and underestimated propensities are expected to give higher bias. Identification requires the propensity to depend on fewer attributes than the classifier ("propensity attributes" x_e). With discrete x_e, SAR reduces to SCAR within partitions, which the authors call suboptimal in practice. SAR-EM jointly fits classifier and propensity. Experiments use eight datasets (20 Newsgroups, Adult, Breast Cancer, Cover Type, Diabetes, Image Segmentation, Mushroom, Splice; Pr(y=1) from 0.11 to 0.55) with PU labels simulated from a propensity model. Reported result: by Nemenyi post-hoc on ranks, SAR-EM is significantly better (p < 0.01) than the naive approach, KM2 and TIcE; the naive approach "sometimes outperforms the SCAR approaches."
- Gong et al., "A recent survey on instance-dependent positive and unlabeled learning," *Fundamental Research* 5(2):796-803, DOI 10.1016/j.fmre.2022.09.019 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11997483/)) [FT, read through a summarizing fetch]. Groups methods into scoring-function approaches (PUSB, SAR-PU, Gong et al.) and Bayesian optimal relabeling (PGPU). States that under selection bias the class posterior cannot be calculated even if α is known, and lists assumption-free or mechanism-adaptive methods as open problems.
- Gerych et al., "Recovering the Propensity Score from Biased Positive Unlabeled Data," AAAI 2022, 36(6):6694-6702, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v36i6.20624 ([Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v36i6.20624); [PDF](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/20624/20624-13-24637-1-2-20220628.pdf)) [FT, introduction]. Notes that unconstrained, "an infinite number of propensity score/posterior class probability pairs can explain the observed PU data."
- Coudray, Keribin, Massart & Pamphile, "Risk Bounds for Positive-Unlabeled Learning Under the Selected At Random Assumption," *JMLR* 24, 2023 ([PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume24/22-067/22-067.pdf)) [FT, abstract and introduction]. Risk bounds under SAR plus a minimax lower bound showing the upper bound is almost optimal; treats PU as a special case of label noise.
- Teisseyre, Furmańczyk & Mielniczuk, "Verifying the Selected Completely at Random Assumption in Positive-Unlabeled Learning," FAIA 2024, DOI 10.3233/faia240675 ([arXiv:2404.00145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00145)) [ABS]. A test for SCAR built from artificially generated SCAR labels, recommended as a pre-processing step.
- Furmańczyk, Mielniczuk, Rejchel & Teisseyre, "Double logistic regression approach to biased positive-unlabeled data" ([arXiv:2209.07787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07787)) [ABS]. Parameters of posterior and propensity are identifiable when both share a parametric form such as logistic with different parameters.
- Rejchel, Teisseyre & Mielniczuk, "Joint empirical risk minimization for instance-dependent positive-unlabeled data" ([arXiv:2312.16557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16557)) [ABS]. Alternating minimisation of a joint risk over posterior and propensity.
- Wang, Chen, Guo & Wang, "PUe: Biased Positive-Unlabeled Learning Enhancement by Causal Inference," NeurIPS 2023 ([PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3efb4bdc6bfe13e1ff95b4407c37961d-Paper-Conference.pdf)) [FT, abstract; author order taken from a garbled PDF header]. Builds on the SAR-PU propensity-weighted framework with normalized inverse probability weighting; experiments on CIFAR-10 and ADNI.
- Hammoudeh & Lowd, "Learning from Positive and Unlabeled Data with Arbitrary Positive Shift," NeurIPS 2020 per arXiv comment ([arXiv:2002.10261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10261)) [ABS]. Says representativeness of positives rarely holds due to temporal drift, domain shift or adversarial manipulation; only the negative class distribution need be fixed, given unlabeled data from both source and target.
- Kato, Wu, Kureishi & Yasui, "Automatic Debiased Learning from Positive, Unlabeled, and Exposure Data" ([arXiv:2303.04797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04797)) [ABS]. Treats positives as a product of exposure and labeling; assumes access to exposure labels and strong ignorability.
- Kumar & Lambert, "Positive Unlabeled Learning Selected Not At Random (PULSNAR): class proportion estimation without the selected completely at random assumption," *PeerJ Computer Science* 10:e2451, 2024, DOI 10.7717/peerj-cs.2451 ([Crossref](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.2451); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11622864/); [arXiv:2303.08269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08269)) [FT]. Uses XGBoost as the base classifier, clusters labeled positives into subtypes, and estimates α (the fraction of positives among unlabeled, not the class prior) per subtype. Assumes unlabeled positives have analogous labeled counterparts; otherwise α may be underestimated. Details in Q2.
- Su, Chen & Xu, "Positive-Unlabeled Learning from Imbalanced Data," IJCAI 2021, pp. 2995-3001, DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2021/412 ([PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0412.pdf)) [FT, partial]. Adapts consistent risk estimators to imbalance (ImbalancednnPU); experiments use CIFAR-10 with one class positive, π ≈ 0.1, 1,000 positives, 50,000 unlabeled. I did not capture per-dataset numbers.
- Zavitsanos & Paliouras, "Focused PU learning from imbalanced data," *Data Min Knowl Disc* 40, 101 (2026) ([arXiv:2605.14467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14467)) [ABS]. Focused risk estimator evaluated under SCAR and SAR; targets hard positives that resemble negatives.
- Furmańczyk & Paczutkowski, "PU classification under Non-SCAR: clustering-assisted logistic model with oversampling enhancement," MDAI 2026 proceedings ([arXiv:2609.14675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14675)) [ABS]. SMOTE plus 2-means label cleaning before logistic regression on 13 real benchmarks and one synthetic dataset.
- Kumagai et al., "AUC Maximization from Biased Positive-unlabeled Data with Confidence" ([arXiv:2609.10928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10928)) [ABS]. Requires confidence values on the labeled positives.
- Liu et al., "In-Context Positive-Unlabeled Learning" (PUICL) ([arXiv:2605.05591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05591)) [ABS]. Pretrained transformer for PU tabular tasks, evaluated on 20 semi-synthetic UCI-derived benchmarks.
- Wilton, Koay, Ko, Xu & Ye, "Positive-Unlabeled Learning Using Random Forests Via Recursive Greedy Risk Minimization," NeurIPS 35, pp. 24060-24071, DOI 10.52202/068431-1747 ([Crossref](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-1747)) [REC]. ### Inferences
- The clean formal model here is SAR with activity volume (and other visibility features) as propensity attributes, because enforcement visibility is what drives labeling. Bekker's identification argument permits overlap between propensity attributes and classifier features, but when the propensity attribute is also the strongest predictor of the class, the data barely separate "unseen" from "not abusive." Expect weak identification and present SAR estimates as sensitivity analyses over propensity specifications.
- First define what 2.2% and 7.6% are. If they are observed disappearance rates, they estimate p(s=1), and the true prevalence is at least that high (under SCAR, p(y=1) = p(s=1)/c). If they are model-based estimates of p(y=1), say which estimator and assumption produced them. Treating the two as interchangeable would corrupt every prior-dependent step downstream.
- The one-sample setting applies here, since labeled and unlabeled accounts come from one population. nnPU is a two-sample formulation, and Wang et al. (2026) show that mismatch degrades performance unless the prior is calibrated.
- nnPU's published evaluation covers priors of 0.40 to 0.50, so it says nothing direct about 2 to 8% positives; Su et al. and Zavitsanos & Paliouras exist because imbalance needs explicit handling.
- Required comparators: naive XGBoost treating unlabeled as negative, Elkan-Noto, PU bagging, and SAR-EM. Bekker's own finding that the naive approach sometimes beats SCAR methods means PU methods cannot be assumed to win.
- The negative weights in the propensity-weighted estimator need care with boosted trees; test that the learner tolerates them or use a formulation without them.

### Gaps
- I found no large-scale tabular PU benchmark and no PU method validated against real enforcement labels with audited ground truth. PU benchmarks simulate labels on small or image datasets.
- I did not find quantitative head-to-head results for SAR-EM against tree-based risk estimators.

## Q2. Class-prior estimation and its reliability under biased labeling

### Takeaway
The class prior is not identifiable from PU data without extra assumptions even under SCAR, and every common estimator encodes one such assumption. Under biased labeling, published evidence shows SCAR estimators drift substantially, and low-prevalence regimes (α ≤ 5%) remain understudied, so the prior should be reported as a sensitivity range.

### Cited Findings
- The class prior is "not identifiable," because a missing label can reflect either a small prior or a low label frequency; identifying assumptions from strongest to weakest are separable classes, positive subdomain (anchor set), positive function, and irreducibility — [Bekker & Davis survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) [FT]; irreducibility means the negative distribution cannot be a mixture containing the positive distribution — [Bekker et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03207) [FT].
- Method families in the survey: non-traditional classifier approaches (Elkan-Noto; these need well-calibrated probabilities, with Platt, isotonic or beta calibration suggested), partial matching, decision-tree induction (TIcE), and ROC-based approaches — [survey §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) [FT].
- TIcE (Bekker & Davis, AAAI 2018, 32(1), DOI 10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11715) uses subdomains to lower-bound the label frequency; its abstract reports accuracy equivalent to state of the art and an order of magnitude faster — [OpenAlex abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v32i1.11715) [ABS]; the survey notes it relies on the positive subdomain assumption [FT].
- KM1/KM2: Ramaswamy, Scott & Tewari, "Mixture Proportion Estimation via Kernel Embedding of Distributions," PMLR 48 — [PDF](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/ramaswamy16.pdf) [FT, abstract and setup]. Embeds distributions in an RKHS and gives convergence rates under stated distributional assumptions, including a separability condition.
- BBE: guarantees hold "whenever we can train a model to cleanly separate out a small subset of positive examples" — [Garg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00980) [ABS].
- DEDPUL claims to outperform prior methods in proportion estimation by post-processing any classifier's predictions — [Ivanov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06965) [ABS].
- Distribution-free methods rely on the positive support not being contained in the negative support; when this fails they "systematically overestimate the class prior," and the assumption cannot be verified from data. ReCPE was proposed for this case — [Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03673) [ABS]; ICLR 2022 per the reference list of [Wang et al. 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/cbffadf96101c42be845d3e275c4b7e9-Paper-Conference.pdf) [2nd].
- A resampling meta-algorithm extends mixture proportion estimation beyond irreducibility — [Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01253) [ABS].
- Under biased labeling: [PULSNAR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11622864/) [FT]. Setting: SNAR synthetic data (make_classification, class_sep=0.3, 2,000 positives, 6,000 unlabeled, five positive subclasses, 40 random seeds, positive fractions in unlabeled of 1 to 50%) and six SNAR UCI-derived datasets (Anuran calls, Dry bean, Room occupancy estimation, Smartphone, Letter recognition, Statlog Shuttle). Reported: "only PULSNAR's estimates were close to the true α"; on SNAR benchmarks KM1, KM2, TIcE, DEDPUL and PULSCAR over- or under-estimated α for some or all fractions, usually underestimating at fractions ≥ 15%; TIcE overestimated at all fractions on the synthetic data. Exact error values appear only in figures.
- Runtime on synthetic SNAR data with 5,000 positives and 100,000 unlabeled (PeerJ Table 2, in minutes): TIcE 706.50, DEDPUL 159.59, PULSCAR 3.19, PULSNAR 14.07; KM failed at this size — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11622864/) [FT].
- The PULSNAR authors say few PU algorithms have been assessed at α ≤ 5%, where performance is expected to suffer; α is not the class prior but the fraction of positives among unlabeled — [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08269) [FT].
- Under SAR specifically: if any instance can have any propensity, a missing label cannot be attributed to low propensity or low class probability — [Bekker et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03207) [FT]; the class posterior cannot be computed under selection bias even with a known α — [Gong et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11997483/) [FT].
- Practical pieces: pulearn offers `LabelFrequencyPriorEstimator`, `HistogramMatchPriorEstimator`, `ScarEMPriorEstimator`, and `analyze_prior_sensitivity` — [pulearn README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pulearn/pulearn/master/README.rst) [FT].

### Inferences
- Treat the 2.2% and 7.6% figures as ranges. Run several estimators with different identifying assumptions and report their spread by activity stratum; a tight spread is evidence of robustness, and a wide one is itself a finding.
- A semi-synthetic check is feasible: hide known positives under an activity-dependent propensity on your own feature distribution (Bekker's protocol), then measure how far each estimator misses. This validates estimators only for the labeled-positive population, not for the abusive accounts that were never caught.
- PULSNAR's α converts to a prior as π = (n_L + α·n_U)/(n_L + n_U). Its positive-subtype clustering is a plausible match for coordinated-cluster structure, but its support assumption fails exactly where an account type was never enforced against.
- Because α ≤ 5% is understudied and low-activity strata have the lowest labeled counts, prior estimates there should be treated as the least reliable.
- A scale check matters: TIcE and KM runtimes in PULSNAR's table suggest subsampling before estimation on GitHub-scale data.

### Gaps
- No study evaluates prior estimators against real enforcement labels with audited ground truth, and I found none at α around 2%.
- PULSNAR's precise α errors are not stated in text; only figures show them.

#### Unverified
- AlphaMax (Jain et al., 2016): described only in PULSNAR's related work; the original paper was not opened.
- du Plessis, Niu & Sugiyama, "Class-prior estimation for learning from positive and unlabeled data," *Machine Learning* 106(4):463-492, DOI 10.1007/s10994-016-5604-6: Crossref record only.
- Scott et al. (2013), referenced by Menon et al. for the mutually contaminated distributions framework: not opened.

## Q3. Evaluating PU models without true negatives, and PU versus class-conditional label-noise methods

### Takeaway
Under SCAR, recall and rank-based metrics estimated on labeled positives are meaningful and precision is a lower bound; corrected ROC and PR curves are available given the prior. Under SAR, these corrections do not apply as-is, so report labeled-positive metrics, sensitivity to prior and propensity, and independent audits of top-ranked accounts. PU learning is a one-sided special case of label-noise learning, and class-conditional noise methods encode a SCAR-like assumption that this problem violates.

### Cited Findings
- F1 cannot be estimated from PU data; recall can under SCAR via Pr(ŷ=1|s=1), and the classic alternative criterion is r²/Pr(ŷ=1), attributed to Lee & Liu — [survey §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) [FT]. Under SCAR, knowing the prior gives the expected number of positives in unlabeled data and similar rank distributions, enabling (bounds on) standard metrics — [survey §4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) [FT].
- Any contingency-table metric, including ROC and PR, can be estimated from the fraction of latent positives β, assuming known positives are a random sample of positives; the paper gives bounds such as a tight lower bound on the FPR at a given rank — [Claesen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06837) [FT, partial].
- ROC and PR curves computed by treating unlabeled as negative are biased; they can be corrected with the class prior, and a further correction handles noisy labeled positives given the proportion of mislabeled examples — [Jain, White & Radivojac, AAAI 2017](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v31i1.10937), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00518) [FT, partial].
- Model selection without negatives: proxy accuracy (PA, requires π) and proxy AUC (PAUC, requires no π, computed between positives and unlabeled), with propositions tying expected ordering of PA and PAUC to true accuracy and AUC; the paper recommends PA and PAUC and notes oracle accuracy with true labels "has actually been widely used" in the literature — [Wang et al. 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/cbffadf96101c42be845d3e275c4b7e9-Paper-Conference.pdf) [FT]. A related result on optimising AUC under corrupted labels is in [Menon et al.](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.pdf) [FT, abstract].
- AUL: an asymptotically unbiased practical estimator of area under the lift from raw PU data without prior knowledge of the unlabeled, designed for extremely imbalanced data — [Jiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09820) [ABS].
- PU counterparts of TPR, FPR and AUC for soft-labeled validation — [Lei et al., Soft Label PU Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01990) [ABS].
- Ready-made PU metrics with a stated SCAR assumption: pulearn exposes `pu_recall_score`, `pu_precision_score`, `pu_roc_auc_score`, `pu_average_precision_score`, and `make_pu_scorer`; its README says inverse-propensity weighting is needed under selection bias — [pulearn README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pulearn/pulearn/master/README.rst) [FT].
- Manual audit guidance: labels "should be verified whenever possible," for example "by manually investigating false positives or a random sample," and instances with uncertain labels must not be removed from test data — [Arp et al., P2](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf) [FT].
- Records for audit design: Bennett & Carvalho, "Online stratified sampling," CIKM 2010, pp. 1581-1584 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1871437.1871677)) [REC]; Brown, Cai & DasGupta, "Interval Estimation for a Binomial Proportion," *Statistical Science* 16:101-133, 2001 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1009213286)) [REC].
- PU versus label noise: PU is "a special case of label noise" in which a fraction of positives are labeled and no negatives are — [Coudray et al.](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume24/22-067/22-067.pdf) [FT]. Class-conditional noise flips labels with a probability depending only on the class — [Natarajan et al., NIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5073-learning-with-noisy-labels.pdf) [FT, abstract]. Menon et al. place label noise and PU in the mutually contaminated distributions framework and show balanced error and AUC can be optimised without knowing the corruption parameters — [PMLR 37](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.pdf) [FT, abstract]. Confident learning builds on the class-conditional noise assumption — [Northcutt et al., JAIR 70:1373-1411, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.12125), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00068) [ABS]; implementation in [cleanlab](https://github.com/cleanlab/cleanlab) (2.9.0).

### Inferences
- A measured precision that treats unlabeled as negative is a lower bound on true precision, because p(s=1|ŷ=1) ≤ p(y=1|ŷ=1) whenever labeled positives are truly positive (a direct consequence of the definitions). "Account disappeared" includes voluntary deletion, so the bound can fail; quantify contamination through the audit.
- Under SCAR, P(y=1|ŷ=1) = P(s=1|ŷ=1)/c for the corrected precision (from Elkan-Noto Lemma 1). Under SAR with e(x) rising with activity, this correction is not constant across the ranking; inverse-propensity weighting is required and the correction inherits every propensity error.
- Recall measured on labeled positives is unbiased only under SCAR. If labeled positives are high-activity and the model favours high-activity accounts, as is the case here, labeled-positive recall probably overstates population recall.
- Mapping the frameworks: SCAR corresponds to one-sided class-conditional noise (flip rate 1 − c for positives, 0 for negatives), and SAR corresponds to instance-dependent one-sided noise. Class-conditional methods therefore encode the same feature-independence that enforcement bias violates, so PU with SAR is the better primary framing. Confident learning remains useful as a diagnostic, for example ranking labeled positives that look unlike other positives.
- A practical audit: stratified random samples drawn from the top of the ranking and from low-score unlabeled accounts, labeled against public evidence by two annotators with adjudication, then estimating precision per stratum with binomial intervals. This gives the only estimate not conditioned on the labeling mechanism, and its cost is the main constraint on sample size.

### Gaps
- I found no published protocol specifically for top-k audits in PU settings with biased labels.
- I found no study that validates corrected metrics against audit ground truth in account-abuse data.

#### Unverified
- Lee & Liu (2003, ICML) criterion: known only through the survey; primary paper not opened.
- Content of Bennett & Carvalho and Brown et al.: records only. Their use for interval choices is my general knowledge, not checked here.
- Ramola, Jain & Radivojac (2019): the candidate DOI I tried resolved to a different paper, so it is excluded.

## Q5. Leakage and evaluation guidance in security ML and applied ML

### Takeaway
Arp et al., TESSERACT and Kapoor & Narayanan jointly require a temporally ordered, group-disjoint, realistically imbalanced evaluation with leakage-free preprocessing and honest baselines. McDermott et al. add that AUPRC is not a default for imbalance; the metric should follow the deployment use, which here is top-k review.

### Cited Findings
- Arp, Quiring, Pendlebury, Warnecke, Pierazzi, Wressnegger, Cavallaro & Rieck, "Dos and Don'ts of Machine Learning in Computer Security," USENIX Security 2022 ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf)) [FT]. Ten pitfalls, with prevalence among 30 top-tier security papers: P1 sampling bias 60%, P2 label inaccuracy 10%, P3 data snooping 57%, P4 spurious correlations 20%, P5 biased parameter selection 10%, P6 inappropriate baseline 20%, P7 inappropriate performance measures 33%, P8 base rate fallacy 10%, P9 lab-only evaluation 47%, P10 inappropriate threat model 17%. Each paper had at least three pitfalls. Selected recommendations: split test data early and store it separately until final evaluation; respect temporal dependencies; do not compute preprocessing over the whole dataset; do not tune hyperparameters or thresholds on test data; delay labeling until ground truth is stable; label inaccuracy should be handled and uncertain instances not removed from test data. The example of P8: at a 1:100 class ratio, 99% true positives at 1% false positives means about 100 false positives for every 99 true positives.
- Pendlebury, Pierazzi, Jordaney, Kinder & Cavallaro, "TESSERACT: Eliminating Experimental Bias in Malware Classification across Space and Time," USENIX Security 2019 ([PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-pendlebury.pdf)) [FT]. Constraints: C1 temporal training consistency (all training objects strictly precede testing ones); C2 temporal goodware/malware window consistency in each test slot; C3 realistic malware-to-goodware ratio in testing. Metric: Area Under Time (AUT), the normalised area under the performance-over-time curve. Published F1 up to 0.99 was shown to be inflated by spatial and temporal bias; on 129K Android apps over three years, AUT(F1, 24m) was 0.58, 0.32 and 0.64 for ALG1, ALG2 and DL, in all cases below 10-fold F1. The tuning algorithm uses only training data.
- Kapoor & Narayanan, "Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science," *Patterns* 4(9):100804, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804. Survey found 17 fields, 294 affected papers, and a taxonomy of eight leakage types; proposes model info sheets — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804) [ABS]. Taxonomy wording, read in the [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048) [FT]: L1.1 no test set; L1.2 pre-processing on training and test set; L1.3 feature selection on training and test set; L1.4 duplicates; L2 illegitimate features (including proxies for the outcome); L3.1 temporal leakage; L3.2 non-independence between train and test samples; L3.3 sampling bias in test distribution. In civil-war prediction, correcting leakage made complex models no better than logistic regression, for example Adaboost's AUC advantage over LR dropped from 0.14 to 0.01.
- McDermott, Zhang, Hansen, Angelotti & Gallifant, "A Closer Look at AUROC and AUPRC under Class Imbalance," NeurIPS 2024 per arXiv comment ([arXiv:2401.06091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06091)) [FT, partial]. Theorem 1 relates both metrics to false positive rates; AUROC favours improvements uniformly over positives, while AUPRC favours improvements on high-scored positives, which suits top-k targets (Figure 1). AUPRC can unduly prioritise higher-prevalence subpopulations (Theorem 3). A review of over 1.5 million papers found the claim of AUPRC's superiority under imbalance is often unsupported.
- Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich & Stitelman, "Leakage in data mining," *TKDD* 6(4):1-21, 2012, DOI 10.1145/2382577.2382579: learn-predict separation, detecting leakage without control of data collection — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2382577.2382579) [ABS].
- Roberts et al., "Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure," *Ecography* 40(8):913-929, DOI 10.1111/ecog.02881: random CV seriously underestimates predictive error on structured data, though blocking may induce extrapolation — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.02881) [ABS].
- Group-disjoint folds: scikit-learn's `StratifiedGroupKFold` keeps groups non-overlapping and warns that stratification may degrade to `GroupKFold`-like behaviour with few large groups — [docs, v1.9.1](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.StratifiedGroupKFold.html) [FT].
- Time-based splits change method rankings in tabular ML — [TabReD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19380) [ABS].
- Model selection overfitting can be as large as differences between learners — [Cawley & Talbot, JMLR 11:2079-2107, 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) [ABS].

### Inferences
What each source requires of this design:
- **Temporal (C1, L3.1, P3).** Fit all feature transformations, encoders and any PU or calibration step on the training period only, with test accounts appearing strictly later. Features must not come from after the observation window.
- **Label maturity (P2, Halawa).** A single 31-month deletion check gives different effective follow-up if cohorts differ in observation date. Hold follow-up constant across train and test cohorts, or censor to a common horizon, and report sensitivity.
- **Group leakage (L3.2).** Assign whole coordination clusters to one side of each split. Define cluster membership from data available at training time, because computing clusters over all periods is itself preprocessing leakage (L1.2).
- **Prevalence (C3, P8).** Keep real class imbalance in validation and test sets; any oversampling stays inside the training fold.
- **Baselines (P6, Kapoor).** Include an activity-volume-only logistic regression. Since activity is known to be highly predictive, beating it is the real test of added value.
- **Tuning and holdout (P5, P3, Cawley).** Run Optuna only on training and validation folds, fix thresholds there, and log every run in MLflow so the single holdout opening is auditable.
- **Spurious correlates (P4).** Activity features that partly reflect enforcement visibility are a spurious-correlation risk, and the model may learn visibility rather than abuse.
- **Lab-only limits (P9).** State that the model is offline and detached from platform review workflows.

### Gaps
- None of these sources addresses PU labels directly. Combining their requirements with PU-specific metrics is left to the designer.
- I did not open a dedicated source on buffer-window sizing beyond Halawa et al.

## Q6. Statistical procedures for comparing models, and clustering the bootstrap

### Takeaway
For one dataset with grouped observations, use a paired cluster bootstrap over whole clusters for AP, precision at k and other non-AUROC metrics, plus Obuchowski-style or cluster-bootstrap variance for AUROC differences. Demšar's multi-dataset tests do not fit a single-dataset design, and Nadeau-Bengio corrections address overlapping CV folds in random resampling, not temporal or clustered dependence.

### Cited Findings
- Demšar, "Statistical Comparisons of Classifiers over Multiple Data Sets," *JMLR* 7:1-30, 2006 ([PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume7/demsar06a/demsar06a.pdf)) [FT, abstract]: recommends the Wilcoxon signed-rank test for two classifiers and the Friedman test with post-hoc tests for more, over multiple datasets.
- Benavoli, Corani, Demšar & Zaffalon, "Time for a Change: a Tutorial for Comparing Multiple Classifiers Through Bayesian Analysis," *JMLR* 18, 2017 ([PDF](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume18/16-305/16-305.pdf)) [FT, partial]: argues for abandoning NHST; the Bayesian correlated t-test models correlation due to overlapping training sets.
- Dietterich, "Approximate Statistical Tests for Comparing Supervised Classification Learning Algorithms," *Neural Computation* 10(7):1895-1923, 1998, DOI 10.1162/089976698300017197: the test of proportions and random-split paired t-test have high type I error and should never be used; 10-fold CV paired t-test has somewhat elevated type I error; McNemar and 5×2cv have acceptable error — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017197) [ABS].
- Nadeau & Bengio, "Inference for the Generalization Error," *Machine Learning* 52(3):239-281, 2003 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1024068626366)) [REC]. Corrected statistic t = mean(x) / sqrt((1/(k·r) + n_test/n_train)·σ̂²), as presented with its uncorrected counterpart in [scikit-learn's example](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_grid_search_stats.html) [FT]; in that example the corrected test gives t=0.750, p=0.227 versus uncorrected t=2.611, p=0.005 (grid-search example on scikit-learn's demonstration data, with 10-times-repeated 10-fold CV).
- Bouckaert & Frank, "Evaluating the Replicability of Significance Tests for Comparing Learning Algorithms," PAKDD 2004, LNCS pp. 3-12 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-24775-3_3)) [REC]. [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_grid_search_stats.html) cites it as the basis for preferring 10×10 CV with the corrected test [FT].
- Bates, Hastie & Tibshirani, "Cross-Validation: What Does It Estimate and How Well Does It Do It?", *JASA* 119(546):1434-1445, DOI 10.1080/01621459.2023.2197686: CV estimates average error over training sets rather than the error of the fitted model, naive CV intervals can undercover, nested CV gives better variance estimates, and for simple data splitting one should not refit on the combined data when producing intervals — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2023.2197686) [ABS].
- DeLong, DeLong & Clarke-Pearson, *Biometrics* 44(3):837, 1988, DOI 10.2307/2531595: U-statistic covariance for correlated AUROCs — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2531595) [ABS]. Sun & Xu, *IEEE SPL* 21(11):1389-1393, 2014, DOI 10.1109/lsp.2014.2337313: fast implementation with linearithmic time — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/lsp.2014.2337313) [ABS].
- Obuchowski, "Nonparametric Analysis of Clustered ROC Curve Data," *Biometrics* 53(2):567, 1997, DOI 10.2307/2533958: extends the DeLong structural components to clustered AUROC; tests assuming independence are inflated under intracluster correlation — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2533958) [ABS].
- Field & Welsh, "Bootstrapping Clustered Data," *JRSS-B* 69(3):369-390, 2007, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x: the cluster bootstrap gives consistent variance estimates under both transformation and random-effect models, unlike the residual bootstrap — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x) [ABS].
- Ren et al., "Nonparametric bootstrapping for hierarchical data," *J. Applied Statistics* 37(9):1487-1498, 2010, DOI 10.1080/02664760903046102: resampling highest-level units with replacement and keeping lower levels without replacement is better than resampling lower levels — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664760903046102) [ABS].
- Cameron, Gelbach & Miller, "Bootstrap-Based Improvements for Inference with Clustered Errors," *REStat* 90(3):414-427, 2008, DOI 10.1162/rest.90.3.414: standard asymptotics over-reject with few (5 to 30) clusters; pairs cluster bootstrap should work in principle, wild cluster bootstrap works better in practice for regression — [abstract via DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.3.414) [ABS]. Cameron & Miller, *J. - Boyd, Eng & Page, "Area under the Precision-Recall Curve: Point Estimates and Confidence Intervals," ECML PKDD 2013, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-40994-3_29 ([PDF](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~boyd/aucpr_final.pdf)) [FT, abstract]: common AUCPR estimators and intervals vary in validity; the paper recommends two intervals that are robust.

### Inferences
- A paired cluster bootstrap, concretely: sample coordination-cluster IDs with replacement (singleton accounts are their own clusters), keep every account in each sampled cluster, recompute the metric for both models on the same resample, and form the interval on the difference. Field & Welsh and Ren support resampling at the top level; this design choice is my synthesis, and it needs enough clusters with positives to behave well.
- DeLong and Obuchowski cover only AUROC. AP and precision at k have no comparable closed form in the sources I opened, so the paired cluster bootstrap is the default for them.
- Demšar's tests assume multiple independent datasets. They are appropriate only if the study genuinely compares methods across several independent cohorts, for example non-overlapping observation windows treated as replications, and few windows give little power.
- The Nadeau-Bengio correction applies to random train-test resampling. With temporal forward-chaining or cluster-disjoint folds, its n_test/n_train term has no direct justification, so use the fold-based result descriptively and rely on the cluster bootstrap over the held-out period for intervals.
- Cluster sizes are likely very uneven in coordinated campaigns, so report the number of clusters containing a positive and check the sensitivity of intervals to the largest clusters. The econometrics results on few and unbalanced clusters motivate this, though they concern regression rather than ranking metrics.
- For the holdout, follow Bates et al.: decide in advance whether the model is refit on combined training and validation data, and do not compute intervals as if the refit model were the one evaluated.

### Gaps
- I found no source validating cluster bootstrap for AP or precision at k differences specifically; this is an extrapolation from regression and AUROC results.
- Block-bootstrap treatment of temporal dependence across windows was not researched in depth.

#### Unverified
- Nadeau & Bengio's and Bouckaert & Frank's texts were not opened; their content is reported through scikit-learn's documentation.

## Q7. Metrics for top-k review with rare positives, and their pitfalls

### Takeaway
Use precision at k and recall at k at review-relevant budgets, average precision as the ranking summary, and AUROC only as a secondary view; report base rates next to every PR-based number. Calibration metrics apply only to P(s=1|x) unless PU correction is performed, and each has estimator-level pitfalls under rare positives.

### Cited Findings
- PR plots can give a more accurate view of performance than ROC on imbalanced data — [Saito & Rehmsmeier, PLOS ONE 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0118432) [ABS]. A curve dominates in ROC space if and only if it dominates in PR space; linear interpolation in PR space is incorrect — [Davis & Goadrich, ICML 2006](https://doi.org/10.1145/1143844.1143874) [ABS].
- AUPRC superiority under imbalance is not a general result; the choice should follow the use case, and AUPRC favours high-score positives — [McDermott et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06091) [FT, partial]. AUPRC has incoherent scale assumptions compared with Fβ, which motivated precision-recall-gain curves — [Flach & Kull, NIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5867-precision-recall-gain-curves-pr-analysis-done-right.pdf) [FT, abstract].
- scikit-learn's AP is Σ_n (R_n − R_{n−1})P_n, not interpolated, and different from the trapezoidal area, "which uses linear interpolation and can be too optimistic" — [docs v1.9.1](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score.html) [FT].
- With ties, a score depends on arbitrary tie ordering; McSherry & Najork compute the average over all orderings efficiently — [ECIR 2008](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/computing-information-retrieval-performance-measures-efficiently-in-the-presence-of-tied-scores/) [ABS]; record DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-78646-7_38.
- AUPRC point estimates and intervals differ in validity across estimators — [Boyd et al.](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~boyd/aucpr_final.pdf) [FT, abstract].
- Accuracy alone is insufficient; ROC alone can look excellent while precision is impractical — [Arp et al., P7](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf) [FT].
- ECE and calibration: ECE is widely used but flawed; rank orderings of recalibration methods change with the calibration measure, and adaptive binning is more stable — [Nixon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685) [ABS]. Equal-mass bins have lower bias than equal-width, and debiased estimators and ECE_sweep are reliable — [Roelofs et al., AISTATS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08668) [ABS]. Modern neural networks are often poorly calibrated, and temperature scaling is effective — [Guo et al., ICML 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599) [ABS]. BBQ is a non-parametric calibration method — [Naeini, Cooper & Hauskrecht, AAAI 2015](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v29i1.9602) [ABS].
- Brier score origin: Brier, "Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability," *Monthly Weather Review* 78(1):1-3, 1950, DOI 10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078<0001:VOFEIT>2.0.CO;2 ([Crossref API record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1175/1520-0493(1950)078%3C0001:VOFEIT%3E2.0.CO;2)) [REC].
- Calibration under imbalance and resampling: probabilities systematically underestimate minority instances despite good overall calibration — [Wallace & Dahabreh, ICDM 2012](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2012.115) [ABS]. Undersampling biases posteriors, without changing the ranking, and requires adjustment — [Dal Pozzolo et al., SSCI 2015](https://doi.org/10.1109/ssci.2015.33) [ABS]. Calibration needs large samples at validation — [Van Calster et al., BMC Medicine 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7) [ABS].
- PU-specific: a classifier trained on labels vs unlabeled estimates p(s=1|x), which equals c·p(y=1|x) only under SCAR — [Elkan & Noto](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/posonly.pdf) [FT], [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) [FT]. PULSNAR reports calibration curves align less well under SNAR than SCAR — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11622864/) [FT].

### Inferences
- Precision at k at a positive rate of 2.2% has a random baseline near 0.022 and a maximum of min(1, n_pos/k). Report both, along with recall at k, whose ceiling is k/n_pos. Tie-break scores deterministically or use the tie-averaged formulation.
- With 7.6% prevalence among accounts with at least 5 events and 2.2% overall, AP and precision at k are not comparable across subsets without a base-rate adjustment. McDermott's Theorem 3 implies AP rewards improvements in the high-prevalence (high-activity) subpopulation, which is exactly the confound at issue, so report by activity stratum.
- Calibration of a PU-trained model is calibration to the observed label process. Without a credible correction, report Brier and ECE as calibrating P(s=1|x) and say so, and use equal-mass or debiased estimators, since positives in the top bins are few.
- Under PU labels, AP, precision at k and recall at k are all computed against a censored label; state in every result table that metrics use the observed labels as truth.

### Gaps
- I did not find a study evaluating calibration metrics under SAR labels for rare events.
- I did not open a dedicated source on confidence intervals for precision at k under clustered sampling.

## Q8. Tree ensembles versus deep models for tabular data

### Takeaway
Tree ensembles remain the strongest and cheapest default on medium-sized tabular data; tuned and ensembled MLP variants have caught up on larger budgets and sizes; foundation models lead on small data in vendor-affiliated reports. Results are sensitive to split design: under time-based splits, rankings change and simple MLP-like models and GBDT do best, which supports the course's baseline set without any model novelty claim.

### Cited Findings
- Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux, "Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on typical tabular data?" NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks, DOI 10.52202/068431-0037 ([page](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/0378c7692da36807bdec87ab043cdadc-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)) [ABS]. 45 datasets at medium size (about 10K samples); tree-based models stay state of the art; a 20,000 compute-hour search per learner.

XGBoost outperforms the deep models tested and needs less tuning; an ensemble of deep models and XGBoost beats XGBoost alone.
- Gorishniy, Rubachev, Khrulkov & Babenko, "Revisiting Deep Learning Models for Tabular Data," NeurIPS 2021 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11959)) [ABS]: no universally superior solution between GBDT and DL. Gorishniy, Rubachev & Babenko, "On Embeddings for Numerical Features in Tabular Deep Learning," NeurIPS 2022 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05556)) [ABS]. ### Inferences
- XGBoost, logistic regression and an MLP are a defensible baseline set, and GBDT's edge on skewed, heavy-tailed features suits event-count data. RealMLP or TabM can be added cheaply if the course allows, but any deep model must be evaluated under the same temporal and group splits, since TabReD shows random-split rankings do not carry over.
- The rare-event PU setting is untested in these benchmarks; none of them has PU labels. CLIMB is the closest on imbalance only.

### Gaps
- No benchmark combines rare positives, time-based splits, group structure and PU labels.
- Several of the strongest 2026 claims come from vendor-authored reports not yet peer reviewed.

## Q9. Which practices are standard, and which combinations remain uncommon

### Takeaway
Nearly every individual ingredient in this design is established practice and should not be claimed as a contribution. The credible contribution is the combination applied to a new problem: a censored enforcement label modelled as PU with an activity-based propensity, evaluated under time and coordinated-cluster splits, with leakage controls and honest uncertainty, and audited against independent evidence.

### Cited Findings
Standard, so not a contribution:
- Temporal ordering of train and test — [TESSERACT C1](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-pendlebury.pdf), [Arp et al. P3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf).
- Group-disjoint splits — [Kapoor & Narayanan L3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048), [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.StratifiedGroupKFold.html); account-disjoint splits are already used in suspended-account work — [Seyler et al.](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18091/18091-28-21586-1-2-20210521.pdf).
- Realistic class balance at test time — [TESSERACT C3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-pendlebury.pdf), [Arp et al. P8](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf).
- Strong baselines, logistic regression and XGBoost included — [Arp et al. P6](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf), [Kapoor & Narayanan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804), [Grinsztajn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815).

- Separating test data until final evaluation — [Arp et al. P3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf).
- Paired tests, corrected resampling, and bootstrap intervals — [Demšar](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume7/demsar06a/demsar06a.pdf), [Dietterich](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017197), [scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/model_selection/plot_grid_search_stats.html).
- PR-based metrics, precision at k, and calibration reporting — [Saito & Rehmsmeier](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0118432), [Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599), [Van Calster et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-019-1466-7).

A SCAR test exists too — [Teisseyre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00145).

Evidence bearing on what is uncommon:
- PU is called relatively underexplored in cybersecurity — [Dilworth & Gudla](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06203). The first PU benchmark appeared only in 2026 and flags that model selection without negatives is non-trivial — [Wang et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/cbffadf96101c42be845d3e275c4b7e9-Paper-Conference.pdf).
- Prevalence of pitfalls in security ML shows even basic rigor is not universal — [Arp et al.](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-arp.pdf) (for example P3 data snooping in 57% of 30 papers, P1 sampling bias in 60%).
- Label lag is handled explicitly only in a few account-abuse studies — [Halawa et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08629), [ATLAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20339).

### Inferences
All of the following are my judgement, based on the searches below, and they are not proof of novelty.
- **Not contributions on their own:** XGBoost, MLP, or logistic regression; Optuna and MLflow; a time split; group-disjoint CV; precision at k; bootstrap CIs; a holdout opened once; the SCAR or SAR assumption itself.
- **Plausibly uncommon combinations:**
  1. Disappearance after a long, fixed horizon used as the positive label in an explicit PU formulation for developer-platform accounts. The closest precedents are Dianping's filtered reviews and Tencent's anti-cheat signals; suspension-based Twitter work largely treats non-suspended accounts as negative or regular.
  2. Activity volume as a propensity attribute in SAR, with sensitivity analysis over propensity specifications and estimators, reporting how conclusions change when SCAR is relaxed.
  3. A single protocol combining forward-in-time splits, cluster-disjoint folds, label-maturity control, PU-aware metrics, and paired cluster-bootstrap intervals. I found no paper combining all five.
  4. A manual or external audit of top-ranked accounts used to check corrected metrics, with the audit design documented.
  5. An activity-only baseline presented as a falsification test, reported by activity stratum so that the AUPRC prevalence effect identified by McDermott et al. cannot hide a volume-only result.
- A modest framing is the most defensible one: the contribution is a credible evaluation of a hard labeling problem, not a new algorithm. Negative or null findings, such as PU methods failing to beat naive XGBoost under SAR, would still be valuable and consistent with Bekker's observation about naive models.

### Gaps
- Absence claims are limited by my search. Queries covered PU learning with selection bias and tabular data, fake and bot accounts with suspended labels, PU benchmarks, fraud detection, spammer detection, malicious domains, class-imbalance benchmarks, and PU with temporal splits and label delay. The search tool's summaries are not exhaustive, and adjacent research notes in this project should be cross-checked before making any novelty claim in the final report.
- I did not verify whether the course or department requires a stricter novelty standard than a thesis-level empirical contribution.
